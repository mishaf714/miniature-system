9 · Methodology

How the numbers are made

Classification

Each review's verified written text is scored for sentiment on a scale from −1 (entirely negative) to +1 (entirely positive). The score reflects what the text says, not the star rating. A 4-star review whose text is mostly complaints scores negative.

  • Positive: score ≥ +0.35
  • Mixed / neutral: −0.35 < score < +0.35
  • Negative: score ≤ −0.35
  • Insufficient text: the headline has no evaluative content (for example "Review" or "-") and no attributable excerpt exists. These records are listed but excluded from every metric.

Scores were assigned by an AI analyst (Claude) reading each text, with a one-line rationale stored per review. They are judgments, not output from a lexicon model.

Contentment score (0–100)

Each classified review contributes points by class. The score is the average:

Contentment = (100·P + 50·M + 0·N) ÷ (P + M + N)
= Positive% + ½ × Mixed%

0 means every review is negative; 50 means balanced or entirely neutral; 100 means every review is positive. The same formula is used for every source, year and group, so scores compare directly. A review's contribution to the overall score is its points ÷ total classified reviews. The interval shown is ±1.96 standard errors of the per-review points. Treat groups under 10 reviews as indicative only.

How data was collected

Collected on . Direct page access to Glassdoor, Indeed, Blind, Reddit, Trustpilot and Comparably was blocked from the collection environment. Records come from web-search index results, which expose each review's URL and headline, and sometimes a summary of its body.

  • Headlines are the reviewer's own words, tied to the review URL.
  • Two URLs were returned with the same body excerpt, so excerpts are used only when they are specific to one review. Conflicting excerpts are flagged and ignored.
  • Dates come from search summaries. A date that conflicted with another review's date was discarded.
  • Nothing is estimated or generated: missing fields read "Unavailable".

Known biases

  • Not a random sample. Search queries covered both positive and negative wording, but the index surfaces a subset. Glassdoor itself reports 4.1–4.2★ and 78–90% "recommend" across 188–734 reviews, which suggests the platform-wide population is more positive than this sample.
  • Most reviews are undated here, so the year view is thin. The Glassdoor review-ID ordering is the better trend signal, with the caveat stated in section 2.
  • Blind has no formal reviews. Its threads are mostly candidates describing interviews, not current or former workers.
  • Glassdoor lists Mercor staff and hourly contractors under one company page; reviewer type is shown where the job title was visible.
